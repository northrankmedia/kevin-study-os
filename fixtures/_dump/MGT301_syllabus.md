# Course Description

An investigation of the major functions of management (planning, organizing, coordinating, and controlling). An in-depth analysis of concepts and theories from the behavioral sciences. Particular attention is given to theories of motivation, organizational structure and behavior, management development, group dynamics, models of authority and the management of changing organizations and how they relate to modern managerial techniques.

The course is all about individuals, groups/teams, and how organizations function.

Students are asked to think critically and assess management and organizational issues. Case studies and application exercises will be used to assist student comprehension. Teams will make case presentations in class.

**Learning Objectives**

The student who successfully completes this course will be able to:

*   describe how people plan, organize, lead and exercise control in business - P.O.L.C.
*   compare and contrast different organizational structures
*   identify attitudes impacting job satisfaction
*   define values organizations hold as important
*   demonstrate an understanding of motivational issues in organizations
*   articulate the foundations of group behavior
*   know the difference between leadership and management

**Artificial Intelligence Policy For this class:**

This course recognizes that AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and others are becoming integral to academic and professional environments. The following guidelines aim to help you use these tools appropriately and ethically while maintaining academic integrity.

**Permitted Uses**

*   **Brainstorming and ideation**: Using AI to generate initial ideas or overcome creative blocks
*   **Editing assistance**: Getting feedback on grammar, clarity, and structure of your writing
*   **Research organization**: Summarizing research findings or organizing information
*   **Learning support**: Explaining difficult concepts, creating study guides, or using practice questions

**Prohibited Uses**

*   **Submitting AI-generated work as your own** without substantial revision, critical analysis, or proper attribution
*   **Using AI to complete exams or quizzes** unless explicitly permitted
*   **Bypassing the learning process** by having AI complete assignments designed to develop your thinking, research skills
*   **Inputting confidential or sensitive information** from class into public AI tools

**Attribution Requirements:** When using AI tools in your work:

1.  Clearly disclose which portions involved AI assistance and which tools were used
2.  Explain how you modified, evaluated, and integrated the AI-generated content
3.  Include a list of all **prompts** you used in AI research / as Sources for your work

**Consequences**

Violations of this policy will be treated as academic dishonesty according to the university's Academic Honesty policy. If you're uncertain about appropriate AI use for a specific assignment, please consult with the instructor before submission.

**Learning Goals:** This policy aims to prepare you for professional environments where AI literacy—knowing when and how to effectively use AI tools—is becoming an essential skill. By learning to collaborate with AI appropriately, you'll develop critical thinking about its capabilities and limitations while maintaining your unique intellectual development.

In addition:

*   **Fabrication.** AI can produce plausible sounding but _incorrect_ information. Don’t trust anything it says at face value. If it gives you a number or fact, assume it is wrong unless you either know the answer or can check with another source. You will be responsible for any errors or omissions provided by the tool. It works best for topics you understand and can verify. Large Language Models (LLMs) (like GPT) fabricate less, but all AIs’ fabricate to some degree.
*   **AI bias.** AI can carry biases, stemming from its training data or human intervention. These biases vary across LLMs and can range from gender and racial biases to biases against a particular viewpoint, approaches, or political affiliations. Each LLM has the potential for its own set of biases, and those biases can be subtle. You will need to critically consider answers and be aware of the potential for these sorts of biases.
