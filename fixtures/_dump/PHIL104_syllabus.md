**COURSE DESCRIPTION**

Ethics enquires into the fundamental perspectives and principles that bear on the evaluation of human conduct. It examines prominent theories about what constitutes a good life, articulates relevant principles of right action, poses basic questions about the nature of morality and engages with various moral problems that confront the individual and society. This course endeavors to acquaint students with traditions of moral inquiry and to equip them with key concepts by which to exercise their own moral reflection. (3 credits)

**PHILOSOPHY PROGRAM LEARNING GOALS**

1\. To help students develop the ability to recognize philosophical questions, including such questions arising within one’s personal, social, and political life.

2\. To help students understand and deploy the concepts, theories, and techniques developed by philosophers past and present to address philosophical questions, including such questions arising within one’s personal, social, and political life.

3. To help students appreciate and incorporate constructive engagement with other points of view on philosophical questions.

4. To help students appreciate the roles of criticism, clarity, and coherence in articulating philosophical positions; to engage in and respond to criticism; and to display clarity and coherence in articulating their philosophical positions.

5. To help students to cultivate the dispositions of curiosity, open-mindedness, and humility essential to philosophical inquiry.

**COURSE LEARNING OBJECTVES**

Objective 1: Accurately describe accounts of moral justification developed by philosophers of historical or contemporary significance.

Objective 2: Apply those accounts to long-standing debates in moral philosophy or novel issues by plausibly interpreting what they do or would say about the debates or issues.

Objective 3: Develop and justify one’s own considered ethical views by providing moral justification of these views, critiquing them, and improving them in light of anticipated objections and counterexamples.

Objective 4: Investigate the metaphysics of moral judgments by (a) considering their truth-aptness, fittingness, and/or distinction from mere preferences or beliefs, AND (b) by discriminating between mere compliance (with professional, legal, or social standards, requirements, or norms) and behavior that is morally justified.

Please remember these learning objectives when you complete the course evaluation. If you understand ethical theory, contemporary applied ethics, and/or the basics of metaethics more after the course than you did before the course, then you learned in and because of this course.
